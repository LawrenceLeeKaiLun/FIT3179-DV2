02 / COFFEE GROWN IN MALAYSIA

Malaysia also grows coffee.

Before looking at imports, let's see how much green coffee Malaysia produces.

CHART 04 · PRODUCTION OVER TIME

Malaysia's coffee production has fallen

How has Malaysia's green coffee production changed since 2020?

What the data shows: Production fell from 4,241 tonnes in 2020 to 2,861 tonnes in 2024, a drop of about 33%.

Source: FAOSTAT, Crops and livestock products, Coffee, green, Malaysia, production quantity, 2020–2024. Production is not the same as imports.

04 / WHAT MALAYSIA SELLS

Coffee also travels out of Malaysia.

Malaysia exports coffee products too. Singapore was its largest reported export partner in 2024. Imports and exports are gross trade flows, so they should not be treated as local coffee production or consumption.

Malaysia also exports coffee products. Who buys them, and how do exports compare with imports?

CHART 11 · EXPORT RANKING

Singapore is the largest export partner in this dataset

We have looked at Malaysia’s coffee imports. Now let’s see which countries buy coffee products from Malaysia.

What the data shows: Singapore has the highest export value in this dataset, at about US$12.3 million.

Only export partners with a value of at least US$400,000 are shown. Values are in US$ thousand. Hover over a bar to see the exact value and quantity.

Next, let’s compare export quantity and value.

CHART 12 · SCATTERPLOT

Exports also differ in value and quantity

We have seen which countries buy the most coffee products from Malaysia. Now let’s compare export value and quantity. Each dot is one export partner.

What the data shows: Singapore has the highest export value and quantity in this dataset. South Korea has a relatively high export value for its quantity.

All 20 export partners are included. Hover over a dot to see the country, quantity and export value.

Some countries also appear in the import data.

CHART 13 · HEATMAP

Some partners appear on both sides of the story

Some countries both sell coffee products to Malaysia and buy them from Malaysia. This chart compares their import and export values.

What the data shows: Nine countries have both import and export records. Brazil only has an import record in this dataset. Vietnam and Indonesia have much higher import values, while Singapore has a slightly higher export value than import value.

Only 10 selected partners are shown. Darker squares show higher value ranges. The colours group values into five ranges. Hover over a square to see the exact amount. Values are in US$ thousand.

Finally, let's compare the size of the two trade flows.

CHART 14 · COMPARISON

Imports are much larger than exports in this dataset

We have seen where Malaysia buys and sell coffee products. Finally, let’s compare the total import and export values in our selected records.

What the data shows: The selected records contain about US$450.0 million in imports and US$36.6 million in exports. Import value is about 12 times higher.

These totals cover 31 import records and 20 export records in our CSV, not all of Malaysia’s coffee trade. Hover over a bar to see the exact value.

Now, let’s bring the coffee trade story together.

Trade data shows where coffee products move, not how much coffee Malaysians drink.
THE TAKEAWAY

A cup of coffee has
a global story.

Malaysia's coffee trade is strongly connected to nearby countries, especially Vietnam and Indonesia. But coffee also arrives from South America, Africa and Europe. The trade data shows where products are bought and sold. It does not tell us where every bean was grown.

05 / ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Sources & notes.

Where the numbers came from

World Bank WITS, using UN Comtrade, 2024. Product HS 0901: coffee whether or not roasted or decaffeinated, husks and skins, and certain coffee substitutes. The source reports values in thousands of US dollars and quantities in kilograms.

Import data Export data

FAOSTAT (FAO), Crops and livestock products, Coffee, green, Malaysia, production quantity, 2020–2024. Values are in tonnes. FAOSTAT data

How to read the charts

Charts use only published country records transcribed into local CSV files. Some small partners are excluded to keep charts readable. Map coordinates are approximate reference points. The world boundary file is from world-atlas, loaded through a public CDN.

All charts and maps are made using Vega-Lite. The maps and charts require an internet connection to load Vega libraries and world boundaries.
